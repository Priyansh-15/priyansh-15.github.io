01 / DISTRIBUTED PROCESSINGPROFESSIONAL WORK

Designing the core engine for distributed billing.

Usage-based billing had to apply configured prices and discount tiers per device or across a tenant's usage. Splitting that work across workers meant preserving the pricing rules and knowing when the report was complete.

I independently architected and implemented the core bill-run engine, from mapping through reporting. I used Cassandra-backed coordination to preserve stage progress across worker interruptions and make recovery part of the execution design.

04 / THINGS I'VE LEARNED

The details are the work.

A few lessons I keep coming back to. None of them are glamorous. All of them matter.

01

Failure paths deserve first-class attention.

Retries, duplicates, partial results, and recovery aren't edge decorations. They're part of the design.

02

Simple to explain is a useful design constraint.

If a change needs too many exceptions to make sense, I usually need to revisit the boundaries.

03

Measurements need context.

A benchmark is meaningful when you can explain the workload, environment, and what was actually measured.
